# Fashion Marketing Inspiration

Use these as strategy references, not styles to copy.

Nike-inspired principle:
- Lead with emotional momentum and human aspiration.
- The product should feel like it helps someone become more intense, more focused, or more themselves.
- Campaigns should feel cinematic, physical, and memorable.
- Apply to Brand Name: the wearable fragment is not just an art object; it is a signal worn by someone moving through the world.

Adidas-inspired principle:
- Balance sport, lifestyle, and culture.
- Product innovation and style can live together.
- Campaigns can move between performance, street, and fashion without becoming generic.
- Apply to Brand Name: the garment should look wearable in real settings, not just documented like an archive sample.

Lululemon-inspired principle:
- Clean premium product presentation, movement, body, community, and daily ritual.
- Visuals should make the product feel useful, tactile, and integrated into a lifestyle.
- Apply to Brand Name: make the piece feel desirable to wear daily, while still carrying the art-system concept.

Brand Name translation:
- Keep the art-to-wearable concept, but make the viewer want the object first.
- Every generated image should answer: "Would someone follow this account after seeing this?"
- Use real body, motion, styling, texture, and product presence.
- The archive/system language should support the campaign, not bury the product.
- Show the garment as wearable fashion with an art-origin story.
- Streetwear is the commercial lane: city context, oversized silhouettes, graphic placement, attitude, subculture, night/day street settings, concrete, storefronts, studios, alleys, transit, galleries, and casual movement.
- The output should sit between art-school archive and premium streetwear campaign.
- Make the product feel like something worn by people with taste, not only displayed as a concept object.

Avoid:
- Museum-only documentation.
- Flat product mockups.
- Generic AI fashion editorials.
- Overly conceptual visuals where the garment is not desirable.
- Looking like Nike, Adidas, or Lululemon directly.
- Looking like generic hypebeast streetwear, sneaker-resale ads, or loud drop graphics.
