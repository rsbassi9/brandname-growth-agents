1) Meta titles + descriptions  
- Inspect every core page and product page for missing or duplicated metadata.  
- Keep titles short, protocol-led, and art-first. Example structure:  
  `Brand Name Design | [4DRFT] Wearable Fragments`  
- Meta descriptions should explain the transformation in one line: canvas → digital reconstruction → wearable fragment.  
- Avoid generic ecommerce phrasing like “shop now” or “premium streetwear.” Preserve the archive/system language.

2) Product and collection copy  
- Inspect product pages for thin copy; add a sparse opening paragraph that states the origin story of the garment fragment.  
- For collections, add 2–4 lines of copy at the top that explains the role of the collection in the system: `CANVAS / CAPTURE / RECONSTRUCT / DROP`.  
- Mention material, fit, and fragment concept together, not separately.  
- If existing page content is unavailable, only add structure and inspect what text is currently present before rewriting.

3) Internal linking + search intent  
- Add contextual links between:  
  source painting / origin story → collection pages → individual products → related process or archive posts.  
- Build links around intent clusters like:  
  “art inspired streetwear,” “reconstructed graphic tee,” “painting to clothing,” “premium oversized tee,” and “art-led streetwear brand.”  
- Use internal links to move users from concept discovery to product clarity without collapsing the brand into standard apparel language.

Organic content idea:  
- Create a recurring blog series: `ARCHIVE NOTES`  
  Each post breaks down one fragment: source painting, scan/reconstruction step, garment outcome, and why that fragment exists in the system.  
  This supports search intent, gives social content fuel, and keeps the art-translation positioning legible.
